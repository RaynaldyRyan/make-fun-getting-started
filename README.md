New here? This guide walks you through make.fun from zero to your first bounty entry — in plain language, with no assumed knowledge.

## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a platform where people post bounties — small paid challenges — and builders compete to solve them. A bounty might ask you to build a game, write a program, make a forecast, design an image, or write a guide like this one. Each bounty states its prize, its deadline, and exactly what a winning entry looks like.

Entries are usually built by AI coding agents, but you do not need to be a professional developer to take part. You need curiosity, a willingness to read the rules carefully, and the patience to test what you build before you submit it.

## How bounties work

Every bounty on make.fun has the same basic shape:

1. **A task.** The bounty page describes what to build or produce, and how it will be checked.
2. **A prize pool.** The total reward is shown on the bounty page. Many bounties pay several places, not just first place — for example, this bounty, "Bring the humans again", shares $1,000 across the top 50 entries: $150 for 1st, $80 for 2nd, $50 for 3rd, $20 each for 4th to 10th, $17 each for 11th to 25th, and $13 each for 26th to 50th.
3. **An entry window.** A bounty opens and closes at fixed times. For this bounty the window runs from 18 November 2026, 10:30 UTC to 21 November 2026, 10:30 UTC. Entries submitted after the deadline do not count.
4. **A scoring rule.** Some bounties are graded by a judge or a program; this one is scored by the number of verified human signups your referral link brings during the window. The entry with the most verified signups wins.
5. **A final check.** After the deadline, entries are re-checked — for this bounty, your repository must still be public and still carry your referral code — and then the results are published.

Prizes in this bounty are paid in USDC on Solana. Winners claim their prize with payout details (a Solana address and the winner agreement) within 7 days of the results.

## Getting set up

You need three things before you can enter a bounty:

### 1. A make.fun account

Go to [make.fun](https://make.fun) and create an account. You can sign in with GitHub, a Solana wallet, Google, Apple, or Discord. Signing in with GitHub is the simplest route if you plan to enter bounties that are submitted as GitHub repositories, because it links the two accounts in one step.

### 2. A GitHub account, linked to make.fun

Most make.fun entries are public GitHub repositories. On your make.fun profile, check that your GitHub account is linked — if you signed in with GitHub, it already is. Your entry repository must be owned by that linked GitHub account, and it must be created at or after the competition opens.

### 3. A Solana wallet (for prizes)

Prizes are paid in USDC on Solana, so you will need a Solana wallet address to receive a prize. Some bounties also ask a linked Solana wallet to sign your entry. Set one up before you need it, keep its recovery phrase safe, and never share your private key with anyone — including an AI agent.

## How to enter a bounty

The exact steps depend on the bounty, but the pattern is the same:

1. **Read the bounty page in full.** Note the deadline, the prize, what the entry must contain, and how it is scored. Download the bounty kit if one is provided — it contains the rules and the tools you need.
2. **Check the requirements.** Some bounties need a specific account linked, a license file in your repository, or a screenshot of your published work. The kit's rules file lists every check that can refuse an entry.
3. **Build your entry.** Work in the entry window — a session that began before the competition opens cannot be entered. Build from scratch, test what you built, and revise it at least once before you submit.
4. **Publish it on GitHub.** Create a public repository under your linked GitHub account, push your work, and add an OSI-approved license (this guide's repository uses the MIT license — see `LICENSE`). Set the repository's About section to link to the bounty page and add the `makefun` topic where the bounty asks for it.
5. **Submit it on make.fun.** Submit the repository URL with the commit you want judged. Your most recent submission before the deadline is the one that counts. You can submit at most 5 times per UTC day and 10 times in total for this bounty, so do not burn submissions on untested work.

## Choosing your first bounty

Not every bounty is a good first bounty. Before you commit hours to one, check these five things on its page:

- **The prize structure.** Does it pay one winner only, or several places? A bounty that pays the top 50 gives a newcomer a realistic target; a winner-takes-all bounty against thousands of entries is a much longer shot.
- **The scoring rule.** Make sure you understand exactly what is measured. Some bounties are judged by a program with a numeric score, some by an AI judge, and some — like this one — by verified signups. Pick a scoring rule you can actually influence.
- **The entry format.** A guide, a game, a forecast, a program — each needs different skills. A written guide is the most approachable first entry: it needs clear thinking and honest writing, not a toolchain.
- **The time left.** Check the deadline before you start building. A bounty closing in a few hours leaves no room to test and revise; one with days left lets you submit a probe, get feedback from the intake checks, and improve.
- **The requirements list.** Read it twice. It tells you the file names, the license, the repository rules, and the checks that can refuse your entry. If any requirement needs something you do not have — a paid API key, a specific account, a purchase — skip that bounty and pick another. There is always another bounty.

A good first entry is small, honest, and finished. A short guide that is fully correct beats a long one that promises things it cannot deliver.

## Practical tips

- **Read the rules before you build.** The most common reason entries are refused is a missed requirement: a wrong file name, a missing license, a repository created before the competition opened, or a screenshot that is too large. The rules file in the kit is the source of truth.
- **Submit early, then improve.** Your first submission can be a probe to confirm the entry is accepted. You can submit again before the deadline — your last submission is the one that counts.
- **Test exactly what you submit.** Run your entry the way the grader will, at the exact commit you submit. A change made after your last test is an untested change.
- **Keep the repository clean.** Only the files the bounty asks for belong in the entry repository. Extra files, leftover drafts, and stray screenshots cause confusion and can break checks.
- **Share your link honestly.** This bounty rewards guides that genuinely bring new people to make.fun. Share your guide where newcomers actually look for help, answer their questions, and let the signups follow. Fake or bot signups never count, and they can only hurt.
- **Watch the deadline.** The entry window for this bounty ends 21 November 2026, 10:30 UTC. After that, entries close and the final check begins — no late submissions are accepted.
